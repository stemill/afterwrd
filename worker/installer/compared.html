How afterwrd is different from Facebook

Facebook is one company holding everyone's posts, photos and habits in one place, and deciding what each person sees. afterwrd has no company in the middle. This page explains the difference in plain words, no technical knowledge needed.

The short version

  • What you post stays on your own site, and only the friends you've said yes to can see it.
  • Your feed is only posts from those friends, newest first. No adverts, no "suggested for you", nothing chosen for you.
  • Nobody makes money from you being there, so nothing is built to keep you scrolling or pull you back.

Who's in the middle

On Facebook, everything everyone does goes through one company. Your posts, your photos, who you talk to, what you stop to look at and for how long: all of it lands on Meta's computers, under Meta's rules, which Meta can change.

afterwrd works more like letters between houses. Each person has their own small site, in their own free account at Cloudflare (a large company that rents out computer space). Friends' sites talk to each other directly, and only once both people have agreed. There's no afterwrd company holding anything, and no central copy of anyone's posts.

Who can see what you post

Facebook

Your friends, depending on your settings, and sometimes friends of friends or anyone at all. Facebook's settings have changed many times over the years.

And always Meta itself. By Meta's own privacy policy, what you post and do is used to decide which adverts to show you and others.

afterwrd

Only the friends you've approved. There's no "public" setting and no "friends of friends": a site can't be seen at all without being signed in or being an approved friend.

Your friends' sites ask yours for your posts when your friends look, and your site checks each one is a friend before answering. If you import your old Facebook posts, those stay private to you and are never shared.

A friend link only lets someone ask to be friends. Even if it's passed on to a stranger, they see nothing until you press Approve.

What's in your feed

On Facebook, what you see is chosen for you. A ranking system picks and orders posts, mixed in with adverts, suggestions and posts from people and pages you never followed. Its aim is what you'll engage with, which isn't always what you asked for.

On afterwrd, your feed is only posts from friends you've approved, in the order they were posted. Once you've seen them, that's it.

Who pays for it, and what that means

Facebook

Advertising. Meta's own annual reports say almost all of its money comes from adverts. Meta says it doesn't sell your data; what it sells is the chance to show adverts to people, chosen using what it knows about them.

More time spent means more adverts seen, and notifications, endless scrolling and autoplaying videos all bring people back and keep them there.

It doesn't stop at Facebook either. Many other websites and apps send Meta information about what their visitors do, using Meta's own tools such as the Meta Pixel. You can see some of what's been sent about you in your Facebook settings, under Your activity off Meta technologies.

afterwrd

Nobody. Your site runs on Cloudflare's free plan, and the code is free and public. There are no adverts, and no one earns anything from how long you stay.

So there's nothing to pull you back. afterwrd never sends emails, or notifications to tempt you in. The only thing it will tell your phone, if you turn it on, is that a friend has invited you to an event, or that one you're going to has changed. While you have it open, it quietly tells you when something new arrives; when you close it, it leaves you alone.

No trackers, no analytics, and nothing loaded from other companies. Your site talks only to Cloudflare, to GitHub (for updates) and to your friends' sites.

A few things on the public record

Not opinions: each of these comes from a regulator, a published paper or Meta itself.

2014

A scientific paper revealed that, for a week in 2012, Facebook had changed what some users saw in their feeds, showing fewer happy or fewer sad posts, to see whether it changed what they posted themselves. It did. The people in it weren't asked individually. The paper.

2018

Meta said the information of up to 87 million people may have been improperly shared with Cambridge Analytica, a political consultancy, mostly gathered through a quiz app that collected details of the people who took it and of their friends. Meta's own announcement.

2019

The US Federal Trade Commission fined Facebook $5 billion for breaking an earlier promise about users' privacy. The FTC's announcement.

2023

European regulators fined Meta €1.2 billion over how it moved European users' data to the United States. The European Data Protection Board's announcement.

To be fair

  • afterwrd is small. Your friends need their own afterwrd site to be friends with you, so it's for the people you actually know, not for reaching everyone.
  • It's your responsibility. There's no company to recover your site if you lose access. Your Cloudflare account is the key, so look after it.
  • Cloudflare runs the computers. Your site sits in your account under Cloudflare's terms, like a flat you rent. afterwrd keeps your posts away from everyone else, but it doesn't hide them from your landlord.
  • Friends are still people. Anything you share with a friend, they can save or screenshot, the same as anywhere.
  • Facebook does things afterwrd doesn't: Marketplace, big public groups, finding people you've lost touch with. afterwrd is for keeping up with the people you already know.
